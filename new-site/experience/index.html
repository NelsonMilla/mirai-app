06

Before you apply.

Nine questions, short answers — open a row for the full one.

01What is the residency?A month inside the popup city · 300 residents · housing, cowork, labs, social life

A month living inside a popup city: 300 curated residents sharing housing, coworking, labs, programming, and social life on a few blocks of Port Island through October. The summits are its two flagship weekends; the other 27 days are the point.

02Do I have to stay all 31 days?No · summit tickets stand alone · the full month gets the most

No. The city runs October 1–31 and the longest stays get the most out of it, but summit tickets stand alone — come for one weekend (Oct 17–18 or Oct 24–25), take both, or apply for the residency and stay the month.

03Where will I sleep?Portopia hotel or hacker houses · Sanctuary · Biopunk · Aevitas · ZuCity

The Portopia, our partner hotel, is a short walk from KBIC; community hacker houses — The Sanctuary, Biopunk House, Aevitas, ZuCity Japan — host residents across the city.

04What does a month here cost to live?Kobe is affordable · lunch under ¥1,000 · housing matched to budget

Kobe is one of Japan’s more affordable major cities — a good lunch under ¥1,000, transit a few hundred yen. Housing runs from community hacker houses to the Portopia hotel; we match accepted residents to their budget.

05How structured is the month?A schedule, nothing mandatory · mornings protected — keep your job running

There’s a schedule — tours, workshops, and two summit weekends — but nothing is mandatory. Mornings stay protected for deep work, so you can keep your job or your company running while you’re here.

06What does an ordinary Tuesday look like?See one day on Port Island · morning work · afternoon labs · evening city

See the daily rhythm: a morning of your own work, an afternoon of labs and workshops, an evening in the city. No two days repeat; most keep that shape.

07Do I need Japanese?No · everything runs in English

No — the residency, summits, and workshops run in English. Learning to order your ramen in Japanese is optional, and recommended.

08I’m here for the science. What access do I actually get?KBIC tours · Minicircle & Shojinmeat workshops · CellHaus lab space on approval

Guided tours through KBIC and the Kansai institutes, hands-on workshops with teams like Minicircle and Shojinmeat, regulators and CROs in the room during go-to-market week — and, on approval, lab space through the CellHaus residency with Kobe University.

09How do tickets and applications work?Residency by application, rolling · tickets $900 · both on Luma

Summit tickets are on Luma. The month-long residency is application-only — we review on a rolling basis, and accepted residents receive next steps by email.
